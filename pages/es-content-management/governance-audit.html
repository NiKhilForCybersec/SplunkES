Detection Ownership, Governance & Audit

📋 Page Status: HARDENED — ES CONTENT MANAGEMENT

Last Audit: 2024-02 | Auditor: Detection Engineering Team

This page defines who owns what, how decisions are recorded, and how to demonstrate detection program maturity to auditors and stakeholders. Every detection needs an owner. Every decision needs a record. Every audit needs evidence.

🚨 CRITICAL: Unowned Detections Are Zombie Detections

A detection without an owner will not be tuned, will not be reviewed, and will not be maintained. It will either generate noise that the SOC ignores, or silently stop working. Both outcomes are worse than not having the detection at all — because you believe you have coverage when you don't.

  • Every detection MUST have a named owner (person or team, not "everyone")
  • Every detection decision MUST be documented (enable, disable, clone, custom, retire)
  • Every detection MUST be reviewed at a defined cadence (quarterly minimum)
⚠️ Assumptions — Verify Before Proceeding
  • Assumption: A detection registry exists (spreadsheet, wiki, or tool). Risk if wrong: No single source of truth for detection state. Create one immediately — even a shared Excel file is better than nothing.
  • Assumption: The SOC and Detection Engineering team have defined roles. Risk if wrong: Nobody knows who tunes, who triages, and who reviews.
  • Assumption: Your organization has compliance requirements (PCI DSS, SOX, or internal audit). Even if not: Governance practices protect you from operational failures, not just audit findings.

The Detection Registry

The detection registry is the single source of truth for every detection in the environment. If it's not in the registry, it doesn't officially exist. If it's in the registry without an owner, it's a risk.

SPL: Auto-Generate Registry Baseline from ES

SPL: Export All Correlation Searches for Registry Initialization 
`comment("Use this to bootstrap your detection registry from ES")`
`comment("Export results to CSV, then add manual fields (owner, MITRE, runbook)")`

| rest /services/saved/searches splunk_server=local
| search action.correlationsearch.enabled=*
| eval status = if(disabled=0, "Enabled", "Disabled")
| eval type = case(
    match(title, "^CUSTOM"), "Custom",
    match(eai:acl.app, "^DA-ESS|^SA-|^Splunk"), "OOTB",
    true(), "Unknown"
)
| eval last_modified = strftime(updated, "%Y-%m-%d")
| table title, type, status, eai:acl.app, eai:acl.owner,
    cron_schedule, action.correlationsearch.enabled,
    action.notable.param.severity, last_modified, description
| sort type, title
| rename title AS "Detection Name",
    type AS "Type",
    status AS "Status",
    eai:acl.app AS "App",
    eai:acl.owner AS "Splunk Owner",
    cron_schedule AS "Cron Schedule",
    action.notable.param.severity AS "Severity",
    last_modified AS "Last Modified"

Ownership Transfer Procedure

When a detection owner leaves the team, changes roles, or when detections are transferred between teams:

  1. Before departure: Outgoing owner documents all detections they own with current status, known issues, and pending tuning actions.
  2. Transfer meeting: Outgoing and incoming owner walk through each detection: logic, known FP patterns, tuning history, SOC feedback.
  3. Registry update: Change owner field in registry for ALL transferred detections.
  4. SOC notification: Inform SOC of new contact for detection questions.
  5. Knowledge test: Incoming owner should be able to explain each detection's purpose, data sources, known limitations, and tuning history.
🚨 If No Transfer Happens

When an owner leaves without transferring, their detections become orphans. Flag ALL orphaned detections immediately. Assign temporary ownership to the team lead. Schedule a dedicated review session within 2 weeks to properly assign and validate each detection.

When an auditor asks about your detection capability, they are asking three questions: what do you monitor, how do you know it works, and what do you do when it finds something? Be ready with evidence for all three.

SPL: MITRE Coverage Summary

SPL: Detection Coverage by MITRE Tactic (from Registry Lookup) 
`comment("Requires a lookup table of your registry with MITRE mapping")`
`comment("Shows coverage gaps at the tactic level")`

| inputlookup detection_registry.csv
| where status="Production"
| eval mitre_tactics = split(mitre_tactic, ",")
| mvexpand mitre_tactics
| stats dc(detection_name) AS detection_count,
    values(detection_name) AS detections
    BY mitre_tactics
| rename mitre_tactics AS "MITRE Tactic"
| eval coverage = case(
    detection_count >= 5, "🟢 Strong",
    detection_count >= 2, "🟡 Basic",
    detection_count >= 1, "🟠 Minimal",
    true(), "🔴 GAP"
)
| sort "MITRE Tactic"
| table "MITRE Tactic", detection_count, coverage, detections

Stop & Escalate Conditions

🛑 STOP and Escalate When:
  • More than 30% of production detections have no assigned owner. Ownership crisis. Assign temp owners to all unowned detections within 1 week. Escalate to management.
  • Quarterly review is overdue by more than 30 days. Schedule emergency review session. If not possible, escalate to management for resource allocation.
  • An auditor asks for evidence you can't produce. Document the gap immediately. Create a remediation plan. Do NOT fabricate evidence.
  • Detection registry does not exist. Stop all other detection work. Build the registry FIRST. You cannot govern what you don't inventory.
  • Staff turnover leaves >50% of detections orphaned. Escalate immediately. Deprioritize new development. Focus entirely on stabilizing existing detections.

Common Anti-Patterns

Anti-Pattern Why It's Dangerous What to Do Instead
"The team owns all detections" "Everyone" means nobody. No individual feels responsible for tuning or reviewing. Assign individual ownership. One name per detection. Team is the backup, not the primary.
"We'll build the registry later" Without a registry, you can't prove what you have, who owns it, or when it was reviewed. Auditors will find this. Build it NOW. Start with the SPL export above. Add manual fields incrementally.
"Counting detections = measuring security" 50 broken detections provide less security than 5 working ones. Count != capability. Measure effectiveness: FP rate, detection coverage, SOC response time, review compliance.
"We don't need reviews — the detections are working" You don't know they're working unless you check. Data sources change, threats evolve, environments shift. Quarterly reviews. Non-negotiable. Even if everything looks fine, the review itself is the evidence.
"Governance is bureaucracy" Without governance, detections rot silently. You discover gaps during incidents or audits — both worst-case scenarios. Governance is protection. The overhead is minimal compared to the cost of a missed detection.
